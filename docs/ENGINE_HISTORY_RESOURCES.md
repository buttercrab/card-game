## Current native ownership

- Payoff history, summaries and full spectator records remain retained. Native Start reserves a supported complete-hand envelope before admitting new growth; staged actions and mandatory Chance preserve a normal-finish runway. Exact archive preflight covers ResetScores/Rematch without deleting older records.
- Session and snapshot producers use borrowed data, avoiding the previous complete-history clones. Each changed Session still transports the entire retained public history; this is bounded retention, not paginated native transport.
- Production socket delivery caps encoding and owns source-byte/frame permits. The separate staged-publication correction below extends the same accounting before checkpoint publication.
- Snapshot encoding is capped before passing bytes to persistence. Restore bounds reads before parsing and validates typed profiles/context/logs before seat allocation or replay. Resource-unavailable files remain preserved, with explicit bounded HTTP507 metadata/upgrade responses rather than invented empty history.
- `RoomClient` parses and retains full Session arrays. Current-room selection prevents subsequent engine projections from traversing them, but cannot undo native parse/retention costs. Connected rooms are not age-evicted merely for growing old.

## Non-destructive hardening options

The smallest compatible capacity policy is an explicit count/byte budget checked before starting another hand or appending ResetScores/Rematch history. It must reserve enough space for the prospective completed record, truthfully refuse further growth at capacity, preserve all retained data and leave accepted gameplay/recovery state coherent. A refusal after a hand has already completed cannot substitute for that reservation. Existing oversize files require an explicit operational status and preservation, rather than truncation or silent deletion.

Restore now has pre-read admission and preserved refusal. Socket queue enforcement alone remains insufficient; native reservations, capped persistence and staged-publication accounting are separate required owners.

Longer-lived rooms need demand-loaded, paginated public history transport and archival persistence ownership. That requires an explicit asynchronous read/recovery contract; a synchronous twelve-row catalog over an already-retained native archive is not that transport. History disclosure APIs must keep their read-policy boundary narrow enough to support that later transport without granting game-command authority.

The bounded retention/reservation policy is enforced in the native backend and qualified by the directed receipts below. Native ingress/control enforcement and final numeric/queue ownership are recorded in the later sections. Whole-engine resource qualification still requires the remaining integrated capacity/performance evidence. Retained records must not be silently deleted or truncated as a shortcut.

### Numeric finish, finite recovery and cleanup

Mighty bid rank/value arithmetic now uses `u16` internally with checked conversion back to the unchanged `u8` contract wire. Maximum 255, no-trump bonus and change-trump edges are covered. The native payoff reserve covers admitted custom rules, rather than assuming default multipliers: a conservative Mighty ceiling of 1,000,000 covers the checked formula envelope; Poker's1,200 covers six 200-chip stacks. Current typed scores, typed historical payoffs/standings and known archived summary `scores`/`history` must remain exact JS-safe integers. Duplicate frozen participant IDs refuse before cumulative booking. Opaque recorded-view extensions are outside this score-field claim. Unsafe legacy checkpoints receive preserved507 rather than rounded score publication.

A normal hand's remaining bounded log-byte envelope conservatively bounds future game transitions, with two additional policy/finish fences. Room-version admission additionally protects one eventual Disconnect per admitted connection. Start reserves 4,096 finite recovery/control revisions; optional metadata and fresh identities cannot spend that spare. Known unchanged-identity bootstrap/reclaim and fresh cancellation can spend recovery spare only above the protected normal-finish/cleanup floor. Cancellation at that floor returns an explicit **nonconsuming** capacity rejection; it does not claim to have fenced the uncertain operation. Cached receipts and Query remain read-only, including away presence. Fresh rejected receipts cannot consume protected runway, and an exact staged game candidate checks its post-log numeric runway before swap.

Connect checks its own revision plus its newly created cleanup obligation; Disconnect spends a revision while removing one obligation. Unknown/duplicate Disconnect is a no-op. Restore reserves the full Connect/Observe/reclaim/cleanup path. Counter exhaustion never wraps or silently clamps an authoritative room revision. Between-hand seat swaps also check their game-revision increment. Directed near-cap tests cover optional metadata spam, unchanged RNG/business state on refusal, duplicate/query away preservation, rejected cancellation, normal completion and guaranteed admitted-connection cleanup. Recovery is finite: the policy does not promise unlimited future connections or successful cancellations at absolute exhaustion.

### Committed entry availability and upgrade races

A bounded per-open-room availability cell contains only the reason derived from the successful checkpoint. Insufficient entry/bootstrap/cleanup runway returns the existing structured HTTP507 `resource_capacity` response with resource `room_version` on metadata lookup and before WebSocket upgrade. A held failed checkpoint leaves the prior cell and original JSON untouched; the new reason becomes visible only after the exact candidate commits. Numeric-unavailable idle retirement preserves the checkpoint and its unavailable identity instead of deleting history.

An HTTP availability read is not a connection reservation. Concurrent upgrades can both observe the earlier committed status. The actor therefore rechecks before inserting a connection or greeting it. A losing upgraded connection receives a bounded `resource_capacity` error, creates no identity/greeting/revision and loses its final owned sender; the existing writer drains/closes, with blocked final drain bounded at 100ms. Its reserved RAII Disconnect is then an unknown no-op. Ordinary close/recovery rechecks metadata and observes the committed 507. Tests cover two contending admissions, released versus spent cleanup/revision ownership, committed 507, held-checkpoint privacy and idle file preservation; they do not claim every admission race returns HTTP507 before upgrade.

### Quiet turns and final queue lifetimes

Already-initialized, unchanged Query/cached receipt/Hint/stale-internal turns skip resource-policy and entry-log recomputation. Initialization, changed/saved business or presence effects, prepared transitions, Chance and explicit resource-status changes still refresh before committed publication. Exact candidate admission is unchanged. A bounded 100-read test plus an actual Hint request/private answer and stale internal result verifies unchanged snapshot/policy/output and zero repeated policy passes; initial setup and an actual reservation refusal still refresh. Changing-turn Session/snapshot measurement is still proportional to retained data: this is not a general mutable-state cache or a throughput benchmark.

Remote worker output uses a 16-entry job queue, a 2MiB encoded job limit, at most 64 pending requests and checked request/connection IDs. Worker reply output uses a 16-entry queue and 64KiB capped encoding. Exact worker-connection guards isolate answers, status updates and cleanup; replacement or cancelled serving aborts the owned writer without affecting its replacement. Cancelled asks remove pending ownership; already queued jobs may still run, but their late answers are ignored. The remote worker client's blocking sends retain its local computation slot through result handoff; blocked writer sends are timed and writer cancellation retires the queue. These are encoded-queue/pending lifetime bounds, not bounds on an already-owned job Value, raw socket frame or computation heap.

Persistence now uses a two-entry channel with awaited save/flush/remove. Its private, non-clone single-actor ownership and save→flush discipline bound the native writer's outstanding candidates; this is not an API-independent multi-producer guarantee. No production `unbounded_channel`, `UnboundedSender` or `UnboundedReceiver` remains in `crates/server/src` at this receipt.

The remaining exclusions are explicit: total process/allocator/TLS/kernel memory, physical RAM sufficiency for arbitrary aggregate configuration, arbitrary unprofiled games, malformed opaque recorded-view extensions, global FIFO/starvation-free cognition, worst-case transport/timing performance and production deployment. Native bounded retention does not replace future asynchronous paginated history delivery. No retained records were silently pruned, and none of these receipts authorizes deployment.
